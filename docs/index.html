What each judge concluded

gpt-oss-120b (OpenAI, open-weight): The evidence points to a severe contraction in active accounts and transaction counts on NEAR, while fee revenue per user and transfer volume per active have modestly improved; however, the market cap is many times annual fees, indicating that price is outpacing on‑chain economic activity and raising concerns about long‑term sustainability.
Would change its mind: If annual fee revenue grew to exceed 10% of market cap (fees.mcap_to_fees dropping below 10), indicating that usage is now funding security at a level comparable to valuation.
DeepSeek-V4-Flash (DeepSeek): The evidence best supports a bearish long-term case: user activity, new accounts, and active contracts have collapsed, fee revenue is negligible relative to token issuance and market cap, and the remaining usage does not justify the valuation. Transfer volume growth and modest supply inflation are positive, but they appear disconnected from sustainable on-chain participation and fee generation.
Would change its mind: A sustained reversal in active accounts and new account creation, with active.yoy_pct and new_acc.yoy_pct turning positive, combined with annual fees growing to a meaningful share of issuance, would change my mind.
GLM-5.2 (Zhipu): The evidence supports a bearish long-term case: NEAR's on-chain usage has collapsed across every activity metric (active accounts -73.5%, transactions -84.2%, new accounts -96.2%, active contracts -97.8%) while its price nearly doubled, and annual fee revenue ($1.2M) is negligible against both its $5.6B market cap (4659x) and $135.8M annual issuance, meaning holders are diluted far more than fees return. Although transfer volume and fee-per-active have risen, suggesting the remaining usage is higher-value, the near-zero correlation between activity changes and subsequent price (lead.corr_active_change_vs_next_3m_price = 0.17) indicates the token's appreciation is disconnected from on-chain adoption, making the valuation fragile.
Would change its mind: A sustained recovery in monthly active accounts above 150,000 for at least three consecutive months alongside rising monthly fee revenue, demonstrating that organic, fee-generating adoption is resuming rather than merely larger transfers among a shrinking user base.
Qwen3-235B (Alibaba): The evidence supports a bearish long-term case: NEAR's core usage metrics have collapsed year-over-year, with active accounts, transactions, and new accounts down over 70%, suggesting a loss of organic network activity. While USD transfer volume and value per user have increased, these gains are overshadowed by the scale of user attrition and a valuation that is extremely high relative to fee revenue.
Would change its mind: Sustained growth in active accounts and transaction volume over the next 6 months, with fee revenue growing faster than issuance, would indicate a recovery in organic demand and economic sustainability.

Context from public sources (checked by an independent audit)

  • Nansen's Q3 2025 NEAR report found one app, HOT Wallet, had more than 100 times the active addresses of the next entity and 56.1% of transactions. That concentration is why account counts swing so hard: Nansen, Q3 2025.
  • NEAR governance passed HSP-027 in 2026 to stop rebating part of gas fees to contract owners and burn them instead, so fees now offset issuance a little more than before: The Defiant. At current fee levels the offset is still under 1% of issuance.
  • Price and USD volume move together, so transfer value is also shown in NEAR tokens (claim C9).

Reproduce

git clone the repository, then make thesis. It downloads the data (NEAR Intents needs a free partner key in INTENTS_JWT), recomputes every metric (out/metrics.json), re-runs the panel (out/panel.json, needs an OpenAI-compatible API key) and rebuilds this page. make numbers recomputes the numbers alone, with no key.
